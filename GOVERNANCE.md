# Non Verba Governance Principles

**Status:** Draft constitutional design  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Intended use:** Public and private repositories

> This document states the governance principles Non Verba intends to preserve as it develops. It is not itself a legal charter. Where these principles need legal force, they must eventually be implemented through the appropriate contracts, corporate documents, ownership structures, and applicable law.

---

## 1. Purpose

Non Verba exists to enable people to request, undertake, and coordinate voluntary assignments while preserving the agency, rights, and legitimate interests of every participant.

Non Verba may pursue revenue, surplus, growth, investment, and financial resilience when these help sustain and advance that purpose.

Financial return is a means by which the organization may remain durable and useful.

It is not the terminal purpose of the organization.

---

## 2. The Three Parties

A Non Verba assignment involves three institutional parties:

1. the **Requester**;
2. the **Operator**; and
3. **Non Verba**, acting only through the roles and powers explicitly granted to it.

Their rights and obligations may differ, but the system must recognize all three.

The foundational invariant is:

> **No coalition of two parties can create a valid contractual state that improperly alters the third party's rights.**

This applies whether the attempted alteration occurs through:

- contract amendment;
- application logic;
- mediation;
- governance action;
- financial pressure;
- side agreements;
- automated decision-making; or
- any other mechanism recognized by the system.

The exact rights of each party are defined elsewhere.

This document establishes that protected rights are not merely interests that can be discarded whenever doing so becomes profitable or convenient.

---

## 3. Rights Before Balancing

Non Verba will sometimes need to balance competing interests.

Some matters, however, should be implemented as **protected rights or invariants**, rather than considerations which management may freely trade against one another.

Where a right is designated as protected:

1. management may not override it merely to increase revenue, valuation, growth, convenience, or efficiency;
2. two parties may not contract around it in a way that improperly harms the third;
3. automated systems must not intentionally create contractual states that violate it;
4. mediation may interpret and apply the right, but may not silently erase it; and
5. changing the right must require the amendment process appropriate to its constitutional importance.

The preferred progression is:

\[
\text{principle}
\rightarrow
\text{explicit rule}
\rightarrow
\text{observable behavior}
\rightarrow
\text{auditable evidence}
\]

Important values should become architecture wherever reasonably possible.

---

## 4. Profit and Surplus

Non Verba is not required to minimize profit.

Surplus can be necessary to:

- maintain reserves;
- survive shocks;
- compensate people fairly;
- develop technology;
- replace infrastructure;
- finance protection and assurance mechanisms;
- expand useful capacity;
- repay or reward legitimate capital;
- remain independent from individual funders; and
- continue operating through difficult periods.

The distinction Non Verba seeks to preserve is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{profit as feedback and fuel}
\neq
\text{profit as the terminal objective}
}
\]

A profitable action is not therefore automatically justified.

Likewise, making a surplus is not automatically contrary to the organization's purpose.

---

## 5. Capital Does Not Automatically Purchase Purpose

Non Verba may someday:

- accept investment;
- issue equity;
- borrow money;
- admit new owners;
- acquire other organizations;
- be acquired in part;
- or access public capital markets.

Such transactions may create legitimate economic and governance rights.

They should not automatically confer an unrestricted right to redefine what Non Verba exists to do.

Any significant financing arrangement should therefore be examined not merely by asking:

> Who owns the shares?

but also:

> **Who can cause a state transition that changes control, purpose, or protected rights?**

Relevant mechanisms may include:

- voting rights;
- board appointment and removal rights;
- veto rights;
- debt covenants;
- collateral and security interests;
- liquidation preferences;
- redemption rights;
- conversion rights;
- side agreements;
- intellectual-property ownership;
- dependency on essential infrastructure;
- emergency-financing provisions; and
- insolvency or default triggers.

Economic ownership and constitutional control are related, but they need not be identical.

---

## 6. The Bargain With Investors Must Be Honest

Mission protection must never depend on surprising investors after they have committed their capital.

Any material constitutional restrictions affecting investors should be disclosed clearly before investment.

The intended bargain should be understandable approximately as:

> You may participate in the economic success of this enterprise according to the rights agreed with you. Your investment does not, merely because of its size, purchase an unrestricted right to redefine the enterprise's purpose or extinguish protected participant rights.

Investors should know what they are purchasing.

Non Verba should know what it is selling.

Neither should depend upon ambiguity.

---

## 7. Economic Ownership and Constitutional Stewardship

If Non Verba becomes sufficiently valuable or important, it may become desirable to separate:

### Economic participation

Rights to legitimate financial participation in the enterprise.

from:

### Constitutional stewardship

Rights concerned specifically with preserving foundational purpose, protected rights, and the integrity of the governance system.

Possible implementations may eventually include:

- different classes of shares;
- stewardship ownership;
- foundations;
- purpose trusts or analogous structures;
- special voting rights;
- independent constitutional bodies; or
- other mechanisms permitted by the relevant jurisdiction.

No particular legal structure is prescribed today.

The design objective is:

> **The constitution should not become purchasable merely because the enterprise becomes valuable.**

---

## 8. The Guardians Must Also Be Guarded

A stewardship system fails if its guardians can obtain extraordinary private rewards by surrendering the principles they were appointed to protect.

Any future stewardship mechanism should therefore account for:

- conflicts of interest;
- compensation;
- self-dealing;
- succession;
- removal;
- inheritance;
- transfer of stewardship rights;
- pledging those rights as collateral;
- capture by investors;
- capture by founders;
- capture by governments;
- capture by large customers or suppliers;
- emergency financing;
- acquisitions;
- insolvency; and
- incentives created by offers to purchase the organization.

Stewards may be fairly compensated for legitimate work.

Stewardship itself should not become a lottery ticket whose largest payout occurs when the steward abandons the purpose.

---

## 9. The Founder Is Not the Constitution

Early founder control may be practical.

Permanent dependence upon a founder's virtue is not the intended end state.

Non Verba should eventually remain coherent if its founder:

- changes their mind;
- makes a mistake;
- encounters financial pressure;
- becomes unavailable;
- loses capacity;
- transfers assets;
- dies; or
- simply ceases to be the best person to govern the institution.

The objective is not to make the founder impossible to overrule.

The objective is to make the foundational principles **independent of any single human being**.

---

## 10. Reserved Constitutional Matters

The following matters should eventually receive stronger protection than ordinary management decisions:

1. changing the fundamental purpose of Non Verba;
2. materially weakening protected participant rights;
3. altering the three-party contractual invariant;
4. changing who possesses constitutional control;
5. converting constitutional rights into ordinary transferable economic property;
6. selling substantially all of the organization;
7. transferring essential intellectual property in a manner that defeats the purpose;
8. dissolving the organization;
9. distributing constitutionally protected assets during dissolution;
10. changing the mechanism by which these constitutional provisions themselves may be amended.

The precise voting thresholds and institutions responsible for these decisions should be chosen later, once the actual legal structure and jurisdiction are known.

---

## 11. Constitutional Amendment

A constitution that can never change may eventually become harmful.

A constitution that can be changed whenever inconvenient protects nothing.

Non Verba therefore rejects both extremes.

Future amendment mechanisms should distinguish between:

### Ordinary rules

Expected to evolve frequently.

### Structural rules

Expected to change cautiously.

### Constitutional invariants

Expected to change only under exceptional circumstances and through a deliberately difficult, transparent process.

The amendment process should make it possible to correct genuine mistakes while making opportunistic capture difficult.

---

## 12. Emergency Powers

Emergencies create unusually strong opportunities for institutional capture.

Any future emergency powers should therefore be:

- explicitly defined;
- limited in scope;
- limited in duration;
- recorded;
- auditable;
- reversible where possible; and
- incapable of silently becoming permanent ordinary powers.

An emergency may justify temporary operational changes.

It should not automatically justify permanent constitutional surrender.

---

## 13. Mediation and Assurance

Non Verba may provide mediation and separately defined assurance or protection services.

These roles must remain conceptually distinct.

Mediation does not give Non Verba ownership over the underlying rights of the Requester or Operator.

Assurance does not automatically authorize arbitrary intervention in an assignment.

Each service should operate according to powers that are:

- explicitly granted;
- limited;
- inspectable; and
- consistent with the three-party invariant.

---

## 14. Transparency and Evidence

Where Non Verba makes important public promises, it should seek ways to demonstrate whether its actual behavior remains consistent with them.

Where practical, governance should move from:

\[
\text{promise}
\rightarrow
\text{measurement}
\rightarrow
\text{evidence}
\]

Possible future reporting may include:

- changes to protected rights;
- constitutional amendments;
- significant conflicts of interest;
- changes in effective control;
- major exceptions made under emergency powers;
- mediation statistics;
- assurance outcomes;
- concentration of ownership or voting power; and
- evidence concerning whether stated protections operate as intended.

Transparency should reveal meaningful institutional behavior rather than merely produce ceremonial reports.

---

## 15. Failure and Dissolution

Governance should consider failure before failure occurs.

If Non Verba can no longer continue its mission, dissolution or restructuring should not automatically transform constitutionally protected assets or powers into private windfalls.

Future legal structures should therefore specify what happens to:

- participant funds;
- outstanding assignments;
- assurance obligations;
- user data;
- software and intellectual property;
- reserves;
- constitutional ownership rights; and
- other protected assets.

The organization should be capable of **failing gracefully without betraying obligations merely because continuation became impossible**.

---

## 16. Jurisdiction Is an Implementation Detail Until It Is Not

Non Verba may learn from many legal traditions and organizational forms.

Possible sources of useful ideas include:

- public-benefit corporations;
- stewardship ownership;
- cooperative structures;
- foundations;
- trusts;
- conventional corporations with protected share classes;
- nonprofit governance;
- constitutional corporate documents; and
- contractual or technical enforcement mechanisms.

Non Verba should not adopt a jurisdiction or legal form merely because it has a desirable name.

When incorporation, investment, or restructuring makes the question concrete, the legal implementation should be chosen according to how strongly it can express the principles in this document.

The question for legal counsel should therefore eventually be:

> **How can these invariants be implemented as strongly and simply as the relevant law permits?**

rather than:

> What values should this organization have?

The values should already be known.

---

## 17. Current Implementation Principle

Non Verba is still young.

It would be premature to freeze every governance mechanism into permanent legal structure.

For now, development should proceed approximately as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{principles}
\rightarrow
\text{contracts and software}
\rightarrow
\text{operational evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{formal governance}
\rightarrow
\text{long-term stewardship}
}
\]

The constitution should grow from what is learned.

Its foundational protections should not be casually discarded merely because implementation evolves.

---

## 18. A Simple Test

When facing a significant future decision, Non Verba should be able to ask:

1. Does this advance or preserve the purpose?
2. Whose rights change?
3. Who benefits?
4. Who bears the risk?
5. Can any two parties impose this outcome on the third?
6. Does this create hidden control?
7. Would the decision remain acceptable if the people making it were replaced tomorrow?
8. Can the result be explained openly to Requesters, Operators, investors, and future stewards?

If a decision survives those questions, it is probably worth considering.

If it cannot survive them, financial attractiveness alone should not make it acceptable.

---

## 19. Final Principle

Non Verba should not depend on unusually virtuous founders, managers, investors, operators, requesters, or future stewards.

Good people are valuable.

Good architecture is what allows ordinary, imperfect people to preserve something worth keeping.

> **Design institutions so that future virtue is helpful, but not required.**
